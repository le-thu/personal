A /now page with life updates

October 2026

In June I canceled my personal Claude Pro subscription and ran on free tiers for a while, out of what I’m now pretty sure was spite. Six months ago I’d written that I was tired of AI and that maybe one day we’d go back to a world without it, though I immediately admitted I didn’t think we would. Then in July I wrote in my journal that we’re basically paid to learn this new tech (work supplies a Claude Premium seat), paid to have fun while we learn and keep our minds sharp.

In August I ran into a former colleague at a wedding who now just links Claude to Figma and reviews the output on his phone. The next evening I owed another team three UIs I’d been pushing off for weeks, so I gave Claude the Figma link and told it to design them for me. I’d been dreading dozens of frames by hand and that’s not really a thing anymore.

By the end of September I was shipping UI at work myself instead of waiting for an engineer. I’m having so much fun ideating and improving things for my site. I have more agency than ever. The fatigue was never really about AI, it was the expectations I piled on myself. Took me six months and somebody else’s workflow to notice that. But can I say AI has profoundly changed my life? I’m not too sure.

I’m also back on ChatGPT for everyday things. The free version now has unlimited usage if it’s text only, and I used it constantly in China for finding restaurants and asking quick questions.

There’s kind of a new worry. I haven’t brainstormed with pen and paper in a really long time. I don’t even know if I can do it as well as before. My handwriting is even worse. I can still generate ideas, just not with my bare hands. Well, kinda, if you count using my fingers to type the prompts that generate the ideas.

I’ve started hating long responses from Claude, even though I obviously need to read them. I tell myself I’ll “review” or “steer” and half the time I’ve just been running with it, only to correct it later.

I’ve gone back to slow journaling, typing entries out instead of dictating them. It does something to my thinking that talking at my laptop doesn’t. Dictation is fine when I’m iterating with AI in a conversation, but writing slowly makes me more deliberate with words and I get to “edit” my thinking as I go.

I also finally tried the thing I’d put on my goals list every year since 2023. A former coworker building a startup needed design help, so in August I started working with them. I didn’t discuss payment on purpose because once someone pays me I have to be serious about it, and I wanted to find out first whether I even liked this. I said I could do 10 to 15 hours a week, maximum. I wanted to be a design partner, not someone just executing tickets.

That lasted about four weeks. The milestone they were going for was basically a revamp of the whole product, so there was a ton to do and it was almost all production work. Meetings at night — and I could have said no to half of them. If I wasn’t in a meeting I was reading docs or designing. My first real task ate an entire Saturday. I could have paced myself and I keep wondering if that would have made it sustainable, but I don’t think I would have. On a Sunday at the end of September I wrote: I feel like I’m getting my life back. It’s hard for me to say I was really enjoying it, and it’s a mix of reasons. I enjoyed talking to the team, but somehow the work itself and the industry didn’t really sustain my interest or curiosity. I don’t want to feel guilty anymore, and it’s just me feeling like I have this obligation while no one is expecting this from me, so why suffer for no reason?

There’s one thing it did: I came back with a renewed sense of appreciation for my day job. I’m having the best time in my career so far and I only really noticed by going and doing something else for a month. I’d still take client work again. At least now I know what I like and what I say no to.

Travel was mostly one big trip, eleven days in China at the end of August. Chongqing, Wulong, Chengdu, Jiuzhaigou, Huanglong. I think Chongqing is my favorite city in China now. Jiuzhaigou looked like it came out of a book about fairy tale places. At Huanglong I could barely breathe because of the altitude so I don’t remember enjoying it much. My fiancée liked it more than I did. Also, we had so much hotpot in Chongqing and Chengdu. After the trip I felt like I could go without hotpot for a couple of months.

I almost forgot about Bangkok in July for the company trip. Three days and two nights is not enough for an international trip, again, but on our last night there was a party bus and we drank and danced until midnight and my thighs were sore the next day. Just like old times. I also got Taco Bell — pretty much the only thing I wanted in Bangkok. I get that the competition is fierce here in Vietnam so I might never see a Taco Bell in Saigon, but I won’t stop wishing.

I also got a camera, and I wrote a whole post about that one. Last Saturday I stopped waiting on the editing and built a draft of the photo gallery for my site instead. Now I just need the photos.

Lastly, books. The one that got me this year was How to Not Know by Simone Stolzoff. I did not expect to cry reading it. The storytelling is so good it felt like watching a series, like I’d met these people and seen what they were going through, and I think it’s the most touched I’ve ever been by a nonfiction book.

I also reread South of the Border, West of the Sun after more than ten years and got captivated all over again, though the main character has not aged well. The first time I read it I thought it was a heartbreaking love story. This time I mostly thought he was a horny, pathetic dude. My first fiction of the year, somehow.

The Elephant in the Brain was interesting and paired nicely with Power, and I started listening to The Righteous Mind on Audible while we were in China, on the train back to Chengdu from Huanglong.

I’ve read 15 books so far. The goal was 16, which I’ll for sure hit, except I read 20 last year so hitting the goal still means reading less. I started way too many books at once and that doesn’t work for me. Going to finish them one at a time.

May 2026

I spent Tet in Hanoi with my fiancée’s family. It was the first year I gave out red envelopes, which made me feel officially adult. I love her family, they’re warm and welcoming, but staying a whole week with them was... a lot. Third day in, I was rolling between the New Year’s feast meals and looking for any excuse to camp out at a café in the Old Quarter for a few hours of air and time for myself. Next time I’m gonna cap it at 4–5 days at their house. I also caught up with a few friends. This year Hanoi was rather warm and only got cold the day we left for Beijing, of course…

November 2025

I recently stepped into a Product Design Lead role — a hybrid role at my company where you split between IC work and people management — and started managing my first Product Designer. I wasn’t expecting a promotion this year, or at least not a track change like this. My goal has always been to level up as an IC, not become a people manager…

May 2025

Why is it that lately I daydream about being on vacation more often than before?

Maybe it’s because I’ve been traveling more and have started to see how lovely it is to truly rest, live outside of work more often, explore, and do my own thing. But what I’m craving isn’t new at all, it’s very familiar: being by the beach in Mui Ne like I did with my family last summer, and slow mornings in Paris with my fiancée. So I know it’s not really wanderlust or a craving for something novel. (I don’t actually like the beach that much, sand gets everywhere... but I do like going to resorts). I love my routine and wouldn’t change a thing, but I’ve been longing for those kinds of moments…
