New Year in China, new apartment, AI fatigue, and a last-minute trip

I spent Tet in Hanoi with my fiancée’s family. It was the first year I gave out red envelopes, which made me feel officially adult. I love her family, they’re warm and welcoming, but staying a whole week with them was... a lot. Third day in, I was rolling between the New Year’s feast meals and looking for any excuse to camp out at a café in the Old Quarter for a few hours of air and time for myself. Next time I’m gonna cap it at 4–5 days at their house. I also caught up with a few friends. This year Hanoi was rather warm and only got cold the day we left for Beijing, of course.

China was something else. Beijing, Shanghai, Wuzhen, about ten days. Great Wall was incredible. Forbidden City and Summer Palace were beautiful. Wuzhen surprised me the most — we did one day there from Shanghai and I had the best mutton noodle of my life, in a little water town I had no expectations about. I don’t know why I waited this long to visit China. What surprised me more than I expected was how futuristic everything is. Cash is essentially nonexistent. You pay for street food, taxis, museum tickets, vending machines, all of it through Alipay or WeChat Pay — a scan or a tap away. The metro system is enormous and immaculate. Restaurants have a QR code on the table, and you order and pay from your phone. Also, I was raking in 15,000–20,000 steps a day. The language barrier was a real thing though — almost nobody spoke English, even in downtown Beijing and Shanghai. My fiancée knew some Mandarin and genuinely saved us a couple of times. Hong Kong was originally part of the plan for the New Year break too, but we ended up using all the time in China instead. It’s booked for the end of May now.

A couple of things happened in March, basically as soon as I got back from Tet break.

Work has been pushing hard on AI, both adopting it into our own workflows and shipping AI features into our products. Tight diverge-converge loops are much harder over Slack than over a shoulder, so the company decided all product squad members need to sit next to each other instead of by department, which meant the design team couldn’t sit together anymore. I was annoyed about the process, but less so after a week once I felt the difference. Third floor, step count up, my desk is next to a big window with plants and plenty of sunlight, can tap a teammate on the shoulder. The tradeoff is fine.

That same weekend my fiancée and I moved out of the District 1 serviced apartment we’d lived in for three years. Rent went up on short notice and we used it as the excuse we’d been waiting for. The new place is an actual apartment — two bedrooms, second bedroom is now a home studio. I bought us each our own standing desk. So far we really love it here. I’d forgotten what it feels like to see a clear skyline from a window. A month in and I still keep forgetting to use the swimming pool.

April was mostly settling in. Then at the end of the month, my fiancée’s brother found himself with a fully booked Phu Quy trip and no one to go with, so he roped her in. She then roped me in. I didn’t need much convincing since I’d never been to the island, and I figured it was a chance to be spontaneous, something I rarely do when it comes to traveling, or anything really. The car left at 11:15 PM, driver going almost dangerously fast, and we pulled into Phan Thiet (the ferry port town) around 1 AM — way too early for the first ferry at 6:15. The waiting area had bunk beds and hammocks you could rent, so we got a few hours of broken sleep, woke up at 5:30, and boarded. Two and a half hours on a speedboat, bumpy the entire way. I’ve done ferry rides before but this one was rough. I was mostly fine until the people around me started openly throwing up. I barely made it off the boat without joining the party.

We got to the island around 9 AM but couldn’t check in until almost noon, so I parked myself at the café right next door — sandy ground, dogs sleeping everywhere, really good vibe — for a couple of hours while we waited. After checking in, we met up with her brother for lunch, then napped for a while. Dinner that night was seafood nearby and I could feel my energy slowly coming back. The second day my fiancée and her brother went out early on their half-day adventure hitting the island’s usual spots while I stayed back at the café, chilling and tinkering with my site. For dinner the three of us drove to the other side of the island to this big restaurant — atmosphere was great, service was a complete mess, food was fine.

The last full day was the highlight. Her brother went on a boat tour, and my fiancée and I finally had a slow morning together before lunch at this seafood place built out over the water on a jetty. They took us to pick a live fish, it goes to the kitchen, comes back as sashimi, head and tail into a sweet and sour soup. Best meal of the trip by far, great view, incredibly affordable. After lunch we went home to hide from the sun for a bit, then rode around to check out a couple of spots, and for dinner her brother ditched us because he’d run into high school friends on the boat tour — same island, same tour, total coincidence — so it ended up just being the two of us with more local seafood. Not a bad ending for a trip none of us planned, and probably the most time I’ve spent with her brother outside of family gatherings.

I caved and bought the Lofree Flow 2 in black with Pulse tactile switches, after months of telling myself I was never going back to low profile or Lofree. Actually, before I got the Flow 2, I got the NuPhy Air75 V3 first — a couple of months after I told myself I was happy with my Keychron high profile keyboard. I loved the Air75 V3, but then the Flow 2 arrived and it just felt like night and day. The aluminum body is gorgeous. The tactile bump is more pronounced than the NuPhy’s linears but still soft, somehow. It’s a sophisticated board and the one I think I’ll keep.

The other thing is I’m kind of tired of AI. Not of using it, but of hearing about it. I still use Claude Code daily, prototype for work with it, and I’m writing this post with it open in another tab. It was way more fun when it was something I played with on my own terms. Now there’s the same amount of work to do, if not more, plus a whole new way of working to learn on top of it. All the constant oversight, too many tools, too many decisions. I wrote in my journal last month: maybe one day we’ll go back to a world without AI. Honestly, I don’t think we will.

I’ve been thinking about my Notion setup. The personal life calendar is going away, into Apple Journal: thoughts, events, fragments, the small stuff. I already journal almost daily anyway, and I don’t need charts and graphs for memories and highlights. What I wrote about my tracking system six months ago is becoming a snapshot-in-time piece, like the keyboard story. I’m fine with it now, and likely most of what I’ve written is becoming this kind of piece.

I published 22 posts last year. This year I’ve had 3 so far. AI tinkering has taken up most of the mental space that writing used to occupy, and I’m still figuring out what that means for the blog.

Some of the books I enjoyed reading these past months were The Art of Spending Money, Today Was Fun, and Tiny Experiments. The latter two were good reminders and ideas, but I went in kind of expecting more depth than what I got. I’m currently reading Misbehaving on Audible.

7 books for the year so far. Goal is 16.
