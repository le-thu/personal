I love traveling and I’m quite boring about it

I love traveling. I’m just not very adventurous about it like I used to be. What some people might call a boring way to travel is just how I enjoy it most.

One thing upfront: I almost never travel alone, it’s usually with my fiancée, sometimes with family, sometimes friends. It’s more about how I stay myself on a trip while sharing nearly all of it with someone else, especially when my fiancée and I want fairly opposite things out of a trip. She’s drawn to the history and culture and landmarks, and while I can enjoy those too, they’re not quite what I’m there for. I’m mostly in it for the food, the tech, and getting a feel for how people actually live somewhere, where they go for a good meal or their coffee, how they get around, the spots they frequent.

Convenience and comfort over most things #

When it comes to planning, I take the flights and the hotel, and my fiancée takes the itinerary. Since she’s into the history and the monuments and what makes a place what it is, I let her run with it and we shape the days into something that works for both of us from there. I’m not big on meticulous planning, so I’m more than happy to leave the map of sights to her and put my own effort into what I think matters just as much: the hotel.

I optimize for convenience and comfort over almost everything else, starting with the neighborhood: ideally we’re next to a metro or subway so it’s easy to get around, with restaurants and things we can walk to nearby. The room itself has to have a window or decent natural light, clean and comfortable, because we’re not the type to be out all day every day. Even if we’re only there to sleep, I want those hours to be good, since sometimes we might decide to stay in mostly and chill.

The airport lounge is part of the fun #

Then there’s the airport. The lounge has become my favorite part of traveling. I have a credit card that offers complimentary lounge access, so I usually get there early, skip the chaos and sit in peace with a coffee instead of hanging around the benches at the gate. The first time I tried one I remember thinking I could never go back, and I haven’t.

I didn’t expect how much an hour before boarding would come to mean. In the last few years, I almost always begin a trip by writing in my journal from a lounge, it’s like a ritual now. There’s something about that quiet time before boarding, coffee in hand, nothing to do yet and nowhere I need to be. It’s a liminal space you could say, not home and not yet where I’m going, one of the few moments where I feel both calm and a little excited, sitting there with a book or writing about how much I’m looking forward to what’s next.

I protect my routine #

Once we’ve arrived, I get pretty protective of my routine, though really on the longer trips. Short getaways take care of themselves since the purpose is leisure, we might take a day trip to see something nearby, but most of it is just relaxing and being comfortable somewhere that isn’t home. On long trips that are five days to two weeks where we move between cities and work through the sights my fiancée has lined up, I have to pace myself: I trade a day of adventures for a slower one, and rarely more than two big days back to back before I need a full day for myself.

A trip longer than two weeks starts to feel long and I can go a little stir-crazy, not because I’m not having fun but because I lose the structure that makes me feel like me.

I keep a little time for myself #

Because I’m always traveling with someone, time for myself is pretty important to me. I don’t mean time alone, more like time where I’m not going to see the next landmark or do anything in particular. If my fiancée or whoever I’m with is happy to slow down too, like a morning or an afternoon at a café or a restaurant, that’s even better. Sometimes that’s tinkering with my site or browsing the internet, sometimes a slow morning just sitting with the atmosphere, watching the streets and the people, taking in what it feels like to be there. To me that’s as fun as any museum or monument, and in a way more real because it’s what’s happening right now. Sure, it might not hold whatever makes a place historic, but it’s the version of the place that’s alive in front of me which is the part I want most when I travel. I get tired of sightseeing pretty fast too, so I keep it to about three things a day before it starts to feel like a chore of ticking places off a list.

Lately it’s also about noticing and finding a small joy in an ordinary moment whether I’m traveling or not, and a new place just makes those easier to catch. Feeling awe in little things seems to get harder the older we get, and I’m thankful I still notice them.

I don’t mix travel and work #

I can’t do the workcation thing where you fly somewhere new to work from your hotel room or a café during the day then explore in the evening. I tried that a couple of times and it has never worked well for me.

If I’m going to travel, I want to actually travel. I can’t sit at a laptop through the day and then go see a place after hours, when there’s nothing much left to see or do, since a new place is meant to be experienced in daylight, when it’s open and alive.

Splitting the day between work and travel leaves me with a watered-down version of both, the value for money doesn’t make sense, so I’d rather have one of them whole.

So that’s how I like to travel. It’s the same life I already love, carried somewhere with a different view for a week or so, shared with the person I’d be sharing the ordinary days with. Then we go home, and I’m always glad to.
