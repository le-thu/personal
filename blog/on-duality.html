On duality

There’s a pattern I’ve been noticing in myself for a while now. I can be happy with how things are and still want something different. I can look forward to a trip, have a wonderful time, and still find myself missing home before it’s over. I have a love-hate relationship with idle time. Too much of it and I get antsy, too little and I get cranky.

These feelings are often at odds with each other. If I’m happy, why am I looking for something else? If I love traveling, why do I want to go home?

I notice it in other parts of my life, too. I want to pick up new hobbies, learn new things, and start projects, but I rarely have enough time to give them all the attention they deserve. I enjoy being a beginner, figuring things out, but I also want to get good at things. I want meaningful work, but I don’t want it to define me. I like having routines — until life starts feeling too predictable.

My first instinct was to find the right balance. To make better choices, manage my time better, or somehow get all these competing desires to settle down. Lately I’ve been wondering if they need to be settled at all.

I’ve been thinking about this in terms of identity as well. I’ve lived in the US for a decade, have a US passport, and think and write in English by default. Yet I identify as Vietnamese without much hesitation. I count in Vietnamese almost exclusively — somehow. I can’t imagine being in a relationship with someone who doesn’t speak Vietnamese. My life has been shaped by two countries and two languages, but I don’t feel like I have to choose between them to know who I am.

I can’t fully explain how all of that fits together. Maybe that’s fine. Not everything about me needs to fit neatly into a category, and perhaps the same is true of the things I want.

I can love the life I’ve built and still long for something else. I can want change without being unhappy with what I have. These feelings don’t necessarily cancel each other out, even when they seem to pull me in opposite directions.

I’m still figuring out what to make of this. I still get restless, take on too many things, and wonder what else I should be doing. Sometimes that feeling is a sign that I need to change something. Other times, maybe I don’t need to act on it immediately.

For now, I’m just going to write about it because naming something is a way of understanding it. I’m not sure there’s a lesson here or a conclusion I’m working toward. I don’t know if I’ll ever reconcile these seemingly contradictory parts of me, or if they even need reconciling.
